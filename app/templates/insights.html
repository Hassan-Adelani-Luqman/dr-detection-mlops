Dataset features — what the data tells us

The labels are imbalanced, and the photographs differ by grade in ways that have more to do with how they were taken than with disease: healthy (grade 0) eyes are mostly small, square, brighter images, while the disease grades are mostly larger 4:3 images. A model can pick these cues up as shortcuts, which fits what we measured: it recognises “No DR” with 89% recall but separates the four disease grades poorly. Colour carries a weaker, more plausible disease signal in the green channel, and sharpness shows no trend, so image quality is not what sets the grades apart.

1 · Class balance

Share of images in each grade, per pool (% of that pool).

What it tells us. Almost half of APTOS (1,805 of 3,662 images, 49%) is grade 0, while Severe has only 193 images (5%), a 9.4 : 1 imbalance; the sight-threatening grades 3 and 4 together are just 13%. The model therefore sees fewest examples of exactly the cases that matter most, which is why the notebook uses class weights and why Grade 3+4 recall (0.39) is its weakest metric. The retraining replay buffer is balanced on purpose (40 per grade), while the held-out evaluation set keeps the APTOS proportions so its scores reflect real-world prevalence.

2 · Image resolution and shape

Megapixels per image: bar = middle 50% of images, dot = median.

Share of images that are exactly square.

What it tells us. Grade 0 images are much smaller: a median of 1.1 megapixels against 4.2–5.1 for grades 1–4, and half of them are exactly square (1050×1050), against 1–5% of the diseased images. Resolution has the strongest link with grade of any feature measured (Spearman ρ = +0.61). That points to an acquisition difference (healthy eyes largely photographed with a different camera or at a different clinic) rather than anything about the retina. The model resizes every image to 224×224, but a square photo still frames the retina differently from a 4:3 one, so shape can act as a shortcut for “No DR”. That would fit the model's near-certain grade-0 predictions and its much weaker separation of grades 1–4.

3 · Brightness and contrast

Inside the round retina (black border excluded), grey level 0–255.

What it tells us. Inside the retina, grade 0 images are brighter (median grey level 96 against 83–87) and have more contrast (18.0 against 14.1–16.2; ρ = −0.31 with grade, the strongest link after image size). The middle-50% bands overlap heavily, so neither separates the grades on its own, and both move with the resolution difference above, which again suggests capture conditions rather than disease. This is why illumination normalisation (such as Ben Graham's method, tested in the notebook) is a standard step for fundus photographs.

4 · Green channel

Mean green intensity inside the retina, 0–255.

What it tells us. The green channel is where retinopathy is easiest to see: vessels, haemorrhages and microaneurysms show the most contrast there. Diseased grades have a darker green channel (median 66–72 against 82 for grade 0), and red is lower too, while blue shows no trend (ρ = +0.06). But green does not keep falling as the disease gets worse (grades 1 to 4 sit at 66, 72, 68 and 72), so on its own it separates “some DR” from “none”, not how severe. Telling the severe grades apart needs the spatial detail of the lesions themselves, which the CNN has to learn from very few examples.
